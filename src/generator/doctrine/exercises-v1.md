# Exercise doctrine — v1

Jack's nuance on individual movements, in his words. The companion to `v1.md`: that file says how to build
the shape of a session, this one says what goes in the slots and why.

This exists because exercise selection was the one layer of the generator with nothing behind it. Every
other rule in `v1.md` came from Jack; selection was inferred from muscle tags and equipment heuristics,
which is guessing.

---

## How this list was built

Counted from the ten programs he actually wrote — 3,490 prescriptions across nine country-club clients and
one home-gym counterexample. The raw sheets carry ~400 distinct exercise *names*, but most of that number
is typing rather than programming: `Assisted Pullups`, `Assisted Pull-Ups` and `Assisted Pullup` are one
movement.

Collapsed by movement pattern rather than equipment, on Jack's instruction — *"most of these exercises are
the same, it doesn't matter if there's a-or something in between, the name is the same for the most part"*
— the vocabulary is **23 movements**. Smith / cable / dumbbell / incline / seated / assisted are modifiers
on a movement, not separate movements. Where a modifier changes the coaching, that belongs in the entry.

So the problem is not 400 exercises, and not the library's 163. It is 23.

---

## How to use this

Two kinds of question below.

**The standing eight** are asked of every movement. They map onto decisions the generator has to make on
its own, and it currently makes all of them by guessing.

**The specific questions** are per movement, written from what the ten programs actually show — a ratio
that looks deliberate, a client the movement is missing from, a variant used once. These are the valuable
ones, because they ask about a decision already made rather than a hypothetical.

Ramble freely. Answers get recorded verbatim; anything inferred rather than said is marked **(inferred)**
so a later reader can tell them apart. **New questions raised by an answer get added back into this file**
— the bank is meant to grow as it goes.

---

## The standing eight

1. **What's the job?** Why does this earn a slot, over the other things that train the same tissue?
2. **Who's it right for, and who's it wrong for?** Age, training age, injury history, body type.
3. **Where does it sit in the session,** and what has to come before or after it?
4. **What reps and sets does it actually get,** and why that range for this movement specifically?
5. **How do you progress it,** and what do you do when someone stalls on it?
6. **What do you swap it for** when the gym doesn't have the equipment — in order of preference?
7. **What goes wrong?** The cue you find yourself giving every single time.
8. **When does it come out of a program entirely?**

## The movements

### 1. Chest press — 331 uses, in 10/10 programs

`Incline Dumbbell Press` 118 · `Chest Press Machine` 71 · `Incline Smith Press` 65 · `Smith Machine Bench
Press` 27 · `Smith Machine Press` 16 · `Incline Smith Bench Press` 10 · flat/multigrip bench variants

- **Incline outnumbers flat roughly 3 to 1.** That looks like a rule, not a preference. What is incline
  doing for these people that flat isn't?
- Free barbell bench appears essentially never — it's Smith or dumbbell or machine. Is a free barbell
  bench something you actively avoid for this demographic, or just not available?
- `Chest Press Machine` (71) and `Incline Dumbbell Press` (118) both show up heavily, often in the same
  program. What decides machine versus dumbbell for a given person on a given day?
- It's the only movement in all ten programs. Is there anyone who shouldn't be pressing?

### 2. Lateral raise — 262 uses, in 8/10 programs

`Dumbbell Lateral Raise` 235 · `Lateral Raises` 37 · `Super ROM Lateral Raise` 6 · `Cable Lateral Raise` 2

- **This is your single most-prescribed movement** — 7% of every set you've ever written, and often
  multiple times a week. What makes it that important?
- Almost always dumbbell; cable appears twice in ten programs. Why not cable, given you reach for cables
  constantly elsewhere?
- What's a `Super ROM Lateral Raise` and when does someone earn it?
- Missing from Carolyn and Jasper only. What's different about those two?

### 3. Biceps curl — 234 uses, in 10/10 programs

`Cable Curls` 114 · `Seated Curls` 49 · `Dumbbell Curl` 32 · `Incline Curls` 17 · `Cable Rev Curl` 14 ·
`Cable Curls (Rope)` 7

- **Cable beats dumbbell 3.5 to 1.** Why is the cable the default for curls when the dumbbell is the
  default for lateral raises?
- When do you sit someone down, and when do you put them on an incline?
- `Cable Rev Curl` (14) — reverse grip. Who gets that and what's it for?

### 4. Row — 208 uses, in 9/10 programs

Row` 16 · `Seated Row Machine` 13 · `Dumbbell Row` 9

- Unsupported dumbbell rowing is rare (25 total). Is that a lower-back decision?
- Missing only from Bella. Why does she get no rowing?

## The absences — what you never prescribe

Three things are missing from all ten programs, and each looks deliberate enough to be a rule.

- **No overhead or shoulder press. At all.** Not one press in 3,490 prescriptions. Your own priority list
  puts chest/shoulders/arms first for men, and lateral raises are your single most-used movement — so the
  delts are clearly not being ignored. Is vertical pressing something you've ruled out for this
  population, and if so, at what point would someone earn it back?
- **No calf work.** Never once. Not worth the slot, or not worth it for these people?
- **No shrugs / direct trap work.** Same question.

Also absent: no hinge except the RDL, no direct forearm work, and no barbell row.
